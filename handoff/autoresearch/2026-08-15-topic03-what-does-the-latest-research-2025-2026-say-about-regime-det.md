# Regime Detection in Equity Markets: Assessing the 2025–2026 Evidence on Transformer and Diffusion Models

## Introduction

Market regime detection — the task of identifying persistent, statistically distinct states of market behavior such as trending, mean-reverting, calm, or turbulent conditions — has become a central theme in quantitative finance as practitioners search for tools that adapt to non-stationary markets ([Mental Momentum AI, 2026](https://research.mental-momentum.ai/r/market-regime-detection-adaptation-cqqklw)). The specific question posed here is narrow and technical: what does the most recent research (2025–2026) say about applying **transformer** or **diffusion** architectures — the model families that currently dominate natural-language and image generation — to regime detection in **equity markets**, and which models are demonstrating the strongest **out-of-sample** performance?

A systematic review of the available 2025–2026 sources — spanning an institutional research paper, an arXiv preprint, academic-adjacent GitHub repositories, and industry commentary — was conducted to answer this. The finding is itself informative and forms the central thesis of this report: **despite the framing of the question, no source in this review documents a transformer- or diffusion-based model being applied to, and benchmarked on, equity regime detection.** Instead, the applied literature in this window is still dominated by gradient-boosted ensembles, Hidden Markov Models (HMMs), CNN autoencoders, and clustering methods. This report documents what regimes are currently identified, which models are reported to perform best out-of-sample, and explains — based on the evidence gathered — why transformer and diffusion approaches remain largely absent from applied equity regime research as of mid-2026.

## Key Finding: A Gap Between the Question and the Evidence

Several sources gesture toward the direction of representation learning without committing to transformer or diffusion architectures specifically. A 2026 practitioner guide lists "Deep Temporal Embeddings" as one of two dominant 2026 trends in clustering-based regime detection, alongside "Optimal Transport / Wasserstein Barycenters," where hedge funds reportedly use Wasserstein barycenters to cluster return distributions across multi-asset portfolios for more tail-risk-aware regime definitions ([Quantopia, 2026](https://www.quantopia.net/ml-finance/clustering-market-regimes/)). Similarly, an industry blog describes "new geometric and representation-learning approaches" emerging "alongside cloud-based AI platforms that deliver regime signals in milliseconds" ([PickMyTrade, 2026](https://blog.pickmytrade.trade/regime-detection-measuring-market-regime-shifts-2026/)). A third source frames the field's evolution as an "architectural shift from classical probabilistic models to advanced deep learning frameworks" but does not name transformers or diffusion models explicitly ([Mental Momentum AI, 2026](https://research.mental-momentum.ai/r/market-regime-detection-adaptation-cqqklw)). None of these three sources cite a specific transformer or diffusion paper, benchmark, or implementation applied to equities; the language is aspirational and forward-looking rather than evidentiary. Two academic-style repositories that do publish concrete architectures — one using CNN autoencoders and Siamese networks ([KangchengX, n.d.](https://github.com/kangchengX/market-regime)), the other using CatBoost gradient boosting ([Hsaadi33, n.d.](https://github.com/hsaadi33/market_regime_forecasting)) — likewise contain no transformer or diffusion component. Two peer-reviewed sources that might plausibly have covered this ground (a ScienceDirect article and two MDPI articles) were inaccessible during this review due to access restrictions, which is itself a limitation worth flagging rather than a basis for inferring their content.

The most credible, recently published equity-specific framework identified — RegimeFolio, an arXiv preprint from September 2025 — explicitly builds on an "interpretable VIX-based classifier" rather than a deep sequence model, and positions itself in contrast to "regime-agnostic models such as DeepVol and DRL optimizers," implying that even deep-learning-based alternatives it benchmarks against are volatility-forecasting and reinforcement-learning models, not transformers or diffusion networks ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14986)).

## Modeling Approaches Documented in the Reviewed Literature

### Ensemble and Gradient-Boosted Methods
The most concretely benchmarked equity-specific system, RegimeFolio, pairs an interpretable VIX-based classifier with "regime and sector-specific ensemble learners (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting)" and a dynamic mean-variance optimizer using shrinkage-regularized covariance estimates, evaluated on 34 large-cap U.S. equities from 2020 to 2024 ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14986)). A separate, smaller-scale project trains a CatBoost multiclass classifier on monthly S&P 500 price features and lagged FRED macroeconomic series, using expanding-window retraining to generate realistic yearly out-of-sample predictions, and reports "improved risk-adjusted performance versus buy-and-hold," though no specific Sharpe ratio or return figure is disclosed in the available material ([Hsaadi33, n.d.](https://github.com/hsaadi33/market_regime_forecasting)).

### Hidden Markov Models and Clustering
HMMs are repeatedly described as the incumbent standard: a Gaussian HMM with three states, decoded via the Viterbi algorithm, serves as an unsupervised baseline in one system, while a supervised ensemble (500-tree Random Forest, XGBoost, and Logistic Regression via soft voting) is layered on top for calibrated regime probabilities ([Saiemula, n.d.](https://github.com/saiemula/Market-Regime-Detection-System)). Industry commentary reinforces this, calling HMMs "the gold standard" and citing a 2023 LSEG framework that combines HMM, GMM, and k-means for multi-regime classification ([PickMyTrade, 2026](https://blog.pickmytrade.trade/regime-detection-measuring-market-regime-shifts-2026/)).

### Deep Feature Extraction via CNN Autoencoders
An MSc dissertation project (University College London, awarded distinction) offers the most architecturally sophisticated deep-learning comparison in this review, testing four unsupervised model families on multivariate financial time series: a correlation-feature model (CFM) using k-means++ and hierarchical clustering; a deep-feature model (DFM) using a CNN autoencoder; a hybrid deep-correlation model (DCFM) concatenating both feature types; and an end-to-end model (EEM) using a Siamese CNN. The DFM most effectively identified the 2008 Global Financial Crisis and the 2020 COVID-19 shock, detecting shorter and more frequent regime transitions than CFM, but at the cost of higher entropy and less stable regime durations. The hybrid DCFM, intended to balance short- and long-term signal, underperformed expectations because correlation features dominated and the model failed to exploit the deep features' representational capacity. The end-to-end Siamese model (EEM) suffered from "class collapse," overfitting to input data while underfitting genuine regime structure ([KangchengX, n.d.](https://github.com/kangchengX/market-regime)). Notably, none of these four architectures is a transformer or diffusion model — the CNN autoencoder is the closest the reviewed literature comes to a modern deep architecture, and it is explicitly reported as introducing more noise and instability than the simpler correlation-based approach.

### Emerging but Undemonstrated: Representation Learning and Optimal Transport
The clearest signal of where the field may be heading — without yet arriving — comes from two industry sources. One cites "Wasserstein barycenters" and "optimal transport distances or eigenvalue entropy for high-dimensional regime mapping" as more noise-robust alternatives to traditional HMMs ([Quantopia, 2026](https://www.quantopia.net/ml-finance/clustering-market-regimes/); [PickMyTrade, 2026](https://blog.pickmytrade.trade/regime-detection-measuring-market-regime-shifts-2026/)). The same PickMyTrade guide references a March 2025 study — "Similarity-Based Regime Detection (Mulliner et al., 2025)" — that abandons predefined discrete states altogether, instead measuring how closely current macro variables resemble historical periods and trading long or short based on that similarity, reportedly producing "statistically significant alpha and positive skewness." This is the single most recent (2025), most specific academic-style citation encountered in the review, though it was only available second-hand via a trading-platform blog rather than the original paper, so it should be treated as a lead for further primary-source verification rather than confirmed evidence.

## Source Reliability Assessment

Sources in this review fall into distinct reliability tiers. At the top is the State Street Investment Management institutional research paper, authored by a named quantitative research lead and subjected to disclosed robustness checks ([Ung, 2025](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/assets/pdf/global/pc/2025/decoding-market-regimes-with-machine-learning.pdf)), and the RegimeFolio arXiv preprint with named academic authors and quantified backtests ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14986)). A middle tier includes the two academic-adjacent GitHub projects — one an MSc dissertation awarded distinction at UCL in collaboration with an asset manager ([KangchengX, n.d.](https://github.com/kangchengX/market-regime)), the other an independent but methodologically careful CatBoost project with explicit lookahead-bias controls ([Hsaadi33, n.d.](https://github.com/hsaadi33/market_regime_forecasting)). The lowest tier includes a hobby project explicitly authored by a "Year 11 student" with zero external validation or backtest disclosure ([Saiemula, n.d.](https://github.com/saiemula/Market-Regime-Detection-System)), and three commercial/promotional blog sources — a quant-guide site, an AI-generated research summary site, and a futures-trading-automation vendor blog — none of which cite verifiable primary sources for their most striking performance claims ([Quantopia, 2026](https://www.quantopia.net/ml-finance/clustering-market-regimes/); [Mental Momentum AI, 2026](https://research.mental-momentum.ai/r/market-regime-detection-adaptation-cqqklw); [PickMyTrade, 2026](https://blog.pickmytrade.trade/regime-detection-measuring-market-regime-shifts-2026/)). This report weights conclusions accordingly, treating RegimeFolio and the SSGA paper as the load-bearing evidence and the blog sources as directional color rather than proof.

## Discussion: Why Transformers and Diffusion Models Have Not Yet Taken Hold

Three factors, inferable from the evidence gathered, plausibly explain the absence of transformer and diffusion architectures in applied equity regime research to date. First, regime labels are inherently scarce and low-frequency — a 30-year US history yields only a handful of true regime transitions ([Ung, 2025](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/assets/pdf/global/pc/2025/decoding-market-regimes-with-machine-learning.pdf)) — which starves large attention-based or generative models of the labeled examples they typically need to outperform simpler baselines. Second, the UCL dissertation's finding that a CNN autoencoder (DFM) already introduces *more* noise and instability than a simpler correlation-based model (CFM) suggests that added model capacity does not straightforwardly translate into better regime stability in this domain, a pattern that would likely worsen with the higher parameter counts of transformers or diffusion networks ([KangchengX, n.d.](https://github.com/kangchengX/market-regime)). Third, institutional and regulatory users of regime signals — asset managers, risk teams — appear to favor interpretability: RegimeFolio's authors explicitly chose an "interpretable VIX-based classifier" over black-box alternatives, and describe their design goal as forecasts and decisions that "remain aligned with current market conditions, enhancing robustness and interpretability" ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14986)). Together, these dynamics favor gradient-boosted ensembles, HMMs, and shallow deep-feature extractors — methods that are label-efficient, moderately interpretable, and already demonstrably competitive — over transformer or diffusion architectures whose primary advantages (very long-context sequence modeling, high-fidelity generative sampling) are not obviously decisive for a problem defined by a handful of slow-moving, low-frequency states.

## Conclusion and Outlook

The concrete, evidence-based conclusion of this review is that **as of August 2026, transformer and diffusion models are not yet a documented, benchmarked part of applied equity regime-detection research** in the sources available. The field's genuine 2025–2026 frontier, based on the most credible sources reviewed, is a VIX-classifier-plus-ensemble-learner architecture (RegimeFolio) delivering the strongest disclosed out-of-sample results — a 137% cumulative return and 1.17 Sharpe ratio across 34 large-cap U.S. equities — alongside continued refinement of HMM/ensemble hybrids and CNN-autoencoder-based deep feature extraction, the latter of which trades regime-detection sensitivity for stability. Two forward-looking threads — Wasserstein/optimal-transport clustering and "deep temporal embeddings" — are the closest analogues to a transformer-adjacent direction currently visible, and the March 2025 similarity-based regime detection study referenced by Mulliner et al. is the most recent lead worth following up in primary form. Researchers or practitioners specifically seeking transformer- or diffusion-based regime detection should treat this as an open research gap rather than an established or benchmarked practice, and should independently verify the Mulliner et al. (2025) study and the still-inaccessible ScienceDirect and MDPI articles noted during this review, as they may contain the missing evidence this report could not retrieve.
